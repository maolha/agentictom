# agenticTOM Editorial Standard

## Voice
Marc Hauser. Senior banking executive who writes with precision. European, not American. Formal but direct. Never academic. Never salesy. The reader is a peer: a COO, CRO, or board member at a Swiss bank.

The closest published references: The Economist's Schumpeter column. FT Alphaville at its most analytical. McKinsey Quarterly when it forgets to sell.

## Audience
Swiss banking and financial services leadership. Many are non-native English speakers with strong professional English. Clear, direct sentences. No idioms that require cultural context. No slang. No jargon that assumes American business culture.

## Scope
This standard applies to all text on agentictom.com: blog posts, homepage sections, page descriptions, assessment questions, results text, CTAs, meta descriptions, and PDF exports.

---

## Rules

### Kill on sight

**Structural patterns:**
- **"Not X, it's Y" / "Not X — Y" constructions.** The single most overused AI pattern. Maximum one per page/post, and only when the contrast genuinely surprises.
- **"The question is not X. It is Y."** Same pattern, different dress. Ban.
- **Rhetorical questions followed by their own answers.** Ban. Make the statement directly.
- **Lists of three with escalating drama.** ("Not just X. Not just Y. But Z." or "Sometimes X. Sometimes Y. Sometimes Z.") Maximum one per page/post.
- **Five or more consecutive rhetorical questions.** Convert to a declarative list.

**Banned phrases:**
- "Fundamentally" / "transformative" / "paradigm" / "game-changing" / "unprecedented" / "revolutionise"
- "The word X is doing real work here"
- "That distinction changes everything"
- "It is worth noting that..." / "It bears mentioning..."
- "In other words..." (fix the first version instead)
- "Let that sink in" / "Read that again"
- "If this resonated..." (LinkedIn-tier CTA)
- "The results may be uncomfortable" / "This might surprise you" (salesy teasing)

**Banned attribution forms:**
- "Research suggests..." / "Studies show..." / "Analysts believe..." / "Sources say..."
- Always name the source: "McKinsey estimates..." / "FINMA requires..." / "CyberArk found..."
- If the source cannot be named, the claim should be labelled as the author's view or removed.

**Consulting buzzwords:**
- "Moat" / "unlock" / "leverage" / "synergy" / "ecosystem" / "disruption" / "north star"
- "Drive impact" / "accelerate transformation" / "future-proof"
- If the concept is real, describe it in operational terms. "Competitive advantage through lower marginal cost" instead of "moat."

**Em dashes:**
- Maximum 5 per page/post.
- Em dashes are for genuine parenthetical asides only.
- When an em dash introduces a list or explanation, use a colon.
- When an em dash joins two independent clauses, use a period.
- When an em dash functions as "that is" or "namely," use a colon.

### Prefer
- **Subject-verb-object.** "The bank processes applications" not "Applications are processed by the bank."
- **Active voice.** Passive only when the actor is genuinely unknown or irrelevant.
- **Periods over em dashes.** Short sentences over long ones with embedded clauses.
- **Specific over abstract.** "CHF 1'500 per application" over "significant cost." "FINMA Guidance 08/2024" over "regulatory requirements."
- **Named attribution.** "McKinsey estimates..." not "estimates suggest..."
- **Varied sentence length.** A long analytical sentence followed by a short declarative one. Five medium sentences in a row is monotonous.
- **Direct CTAs.** "Start a conversation" over "If this resonated, reach out." State the action without qualifying whether the reader should feel moved first.

### Numbers
- Swiss formatting: 20'000 not 20,000.
- Spell out one through ten. Use digits for 11+.
- Always include the unit, timeframe, and source for statistics.
- If a statistic lacks context (denominator, sample size, region), flag it or remove it.
- **Illustrative figures must be labelled.** If a number is modelled, estimated, or illustrative rather than sourced from research, say so explicitly: "Based on industry estimates" or "Illustrative calculation based on..."
- Percentages from consulting reports need the report name and year.

### Bolding
- Bold the takeaway, not the setup.
- A reader scanning only the bold text should get the full argument.
- Maximum one bold phrase per paragraph. Zero is fine.
- On the homepage, bolding should work at scanning speed: a C-level scrolling in 30 seconds should catch the argument from bold text alone.

### Cross-references
- Link to other posts where the topic is genuinely explored in depth.
- The link text should be natural, part of the sentence. Never "read more here" or "click here."
- Only link to posts that are live (date <= today).
- When listing links (e.g., site map section), use colons not em dashes to introduce descriptions.

### Regulatory references
- Always cite the specific regulation: FINMA Guidance 08/2024, FinSA Art. 12-13, EU AI Act.
- Never use vague "regulatory requirements" when a specific source exists.
- Every claim about what AI agents can do autonomously must state the regulatory conditions under which it is permitted.
- If a statistic is attributed to a source, the source must be real and verifiable.
- When contrasting Swiss and EU regulation, be specific about what each framework requires. "Principles-based" vs. "rules-based" needs operational detail.

### Structure
- Open strong. The first two sentences should make the reader decide to continue or forward.
- Each section should end on the implication, not a summary of what was just said.
- The last line of the post should be the one the reader remembers.
- Vary section rhythm: a short punchy section after a long analytical one.
- Avoid identical section structures in consecutive sections. If section 1 is: label → heading → paragraph → quote, section 2 should use a different shape.

### Tone guardrails
- Never tease the reader ("this might surprise you," "the results may be uncomfortable"). State the finding directly and let the reader decide whether they are surprised.
- Never use false urgency ("act now," "the window is closing"). If urgency is real, demonstrate it with evidence.
- Never praise the reader's intelligence or flatter them. The audience is senior. They do not need to be told they are smart.
- Never apologise for complexity or length. If it is worth writing, it is worth reading.
- Avoid "we" when the author means "I" or "you." Marc writes as himself. The reader is addressed directly.

### What not to fix
- Marc's European English (organisation, favour, colour, honour).
- Formal register. This is a framework for executives, not a blog for developers.
- Long paragraphs when the argument requires sustained reasoning.
- Domain-specific terminology the audience knows (TOM, DORA, FinSA, LTV, NHI, RPA, KYC, AML).
- Sentences that end on a period instead of a punchline. Not every paragraph needs to land.

## Review checklist

When reviewing any text on the site, check:

1. [ ] Count "not X, it's Y" constructions. Max 1 per page/post.
2. [ ] Count em dashes. Max 5 per page/post.
3. [ ] Search for banned words and phrases. Should be 0.
4. [ ] Every statistic has a named source, unit, and timeframe.
5. [ ] Every illustrative figure is labelled as such.
6. [ ] Every regulatory claim cites the specific regulation.
7. [ ] Every autonomous capability claim states conditions.
8. [ ] No vague attribution ("research suggests," "studies show").
9. [ ] No salesy teasing or false urgency.
10. [ ] No consulting buzzwords used as substance.
11. [ ] Bold text alone tells the argument (for posts and homepage).
12. [ ] Opening two sentences are strong enough to forward to a board member.
13. [ ] Closing line is memorable.
14. [ ] No AI writing tics survived.
