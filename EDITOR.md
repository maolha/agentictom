# agenticTOM Editorial Standard

## Voice
Marc Hauser. Senior banking executive who writes with precision. European, not American. Formal but direct. Never academic. Never salesy. The reader is a peer — a COO, CRO, or board member at a Swiss bank.

The closest published references: The Economist's Schumpeter column. FT Alphaville at its most analytical. McKinsey Quarterly when it forgets to sell.

## Audience
Swiss banking and financial services leadership. Many are non-native English speakers with strong professional English. Clear, direct sentences. No idioms that require cultural context. No slang.

## Rules

### Kill on sight
- **"Not X, it's Y" / "Not X — Y" constructions.** The single most overused AI pattern. One per post maximum, and only when the contrast genuinely surprises.
- **"The question is not X. It is Y."** Same pattern, different dress.
- **Em dashes used as colons.** Use a colon, a period, or restructure. Em dashes are for genuine parenthetical asides only. Maximum 5 per post.
- **"Fundamentally" / "transformative" / "paradigm" / "game-changing" / "unprecedented."** Ban. If the argument is strong, it does not need amplifiers.
- **"The word X is doing real work here."** Ban.
- **"That distinction changes everything."** Ban.
- **Rhetorical questions followed by their own answers.** Ban. Make the statement directly.
- **Lists of three with escalating drama.** ("Not just X. Not just Y. But Z.") Once per post maximum.
- **"It is worth noting that..."** / **"It bears mentioning..."** Just say it.
- **"In other words..."** If you need to rephrase, the first version was unclear. Fix the first version.

### Prefer
- **Subject-verb-object.** "The bank processes applications" not "Applications are processed by the bank."
- **Active voice.** Passive only when the actor is genuinely unknown or irrelevant.
- **Periods over em dashes.** Short sentences over long ones with embedded clauses.
- **Specific over abstract.** "CHF 1'500 per application" over "significant cost." "FINMA Guidance 08/2024" over "regulatory requirements."
- **Attribution.** "McKinsey estimates..." not "estimates suggest..." "FINMA requires..." not "regulations require..."
- **Varied sentence length.** A long analytical sentence followed by a short declarative one. Not five medium sentences in a row.

### Numbers
- Swiss formatting: 20'000 not 20,000
- Spell out one through ten. Use digits for 11+.
- Always include the unit, timeframe, and source for statistics.
- If a statistic lacks context (denominator, sample size, region), flag it or remove it.

### Bolding
- Bold the takeaway, not the setup.
- A reader scanning only the bold text should get the full argument.
- Maximum one bold phrase per paragraph. Zero is fine.

### Cross-references
- Link to other posts where the topic is genuinely explored in depth.
- The link text should be natural — part of the sentence, not "read more here."
- Only link to posts that are live (date <= today).

### Regulatory references
- Always cite the specific regulation: FINMA Guidance 08/2024, FinSA Art. 12-13, EU AI Act.
- Never use vague "regulatory requirements" when a specific source exists.
- Every autonomous capability claim must state the conditions under which it is permitted.
- If a statistic is attributed to a source, the source must be real and verifiable.

### Structure
- Open strong. The first two sentences should make the reader decide to continue.
- Each section should end on the implication, not a summary.
- The last line of the post should be the one the reader remembers.
- Vary section rhythm: a short punchy section after a long analytical one.

### What not to fix
- Marc's European English (organisation not organization, favour not favor).
- Formal register. This is not a blog for developers. It is a framework for executives.
- Long paragraphs when the argument requires sustained reasoning.
- Domain-specific terminology (TOM, DORA, FinSA, LTV, NHI) — the audience knows these.

## Review checklist
When reviewing a post, check:

1. [ ] Count "not X, it's Y" constructions. Max 1.
2. [ ] Count em dashes. Max 5.
3. [ ] Count "fundamentally" / "transformative" / "paradigm." Should be 0.
4. [ ] Every statistic has a source, unit, and timeframe.
5. [ ] Every regulatory claim cites the specific regulation.
6. [ ] Every autonomous capability claim states conditions.
7. [ ] Bold text alone tells the argument.
8. [ ] Opening two sentences are strong enough to forward.
9. [ ] Closing line is memorable.
10. [ ] No AI writing tics survived.
