# A Staff of Fifteen

Picture every one of your clients with a staff of 15 specialists who work for nobody else. An advisor with a book of 200 clients (an illustrative figure) then faces 3'000 experts across the table, **and none of them ever gets tired.**

This post is a thought experiment. It describes one possible future and asks you to run it against the company you work for.

## The staff

In October 2024, Dario Amodei, chief executive of Anthropic, described what powerful AI could look like: systems "smarter than a Nobel Prize winner across most relevant fields", able to work on a task for days or weeks, running in millions of copies. He called it "a country of geniuses in a datacenter" (Machines of Loving Grace).

Take a small delegation from that country and assign it to one person.

A lawyer. A tax adviser. An investment analyst. A wealth planner. A pension specialist who knows the second pillar and the 3a rules by heart. An insurance expert. A mortgage and property specialist. A negotiator. A strategist who thinks about the next ten years. And a personal assistant who has been there from the beginning, knows every document, every conversation and every promise, and forgets none of them.

They take no holidays and are never ill. They send no invoice by the hour, so no question is too small to ask. **They have one client and one objective: that this person does well.**

Until now, only a client with a family office had anything comparable. [The family office for everyone](/blog/the-family-office-for-everyone) looked at this from the bank's side, as a service the bank can offer. Here the staff belongs to the client, and the bank does not choose what it looks at.

## A scenario, with evidence

Nothing guarantees this future. The staff does not exist at that level today, and regulation, liability and cost may slow its arrival. I hold it as a scenario. The trend is what makes it one a bank cannot set aside.

METR, an independent research organisation, measures the length of software task an AI agent completes with 50 percent reliability, expressed in the time a human expert would need (METR, Time Horizon 1.1, January 2026). GPT-4 reached about four minutes in March 2023. Claude Opus 4.6 reached about 12 hours in February 2026, with a wide confidence interval. Since 2023 the measure has doubled roughly every 131 days. METR notes that its task suite cannot reliably measure beyond 16 hours, and in September 2026 it stopped updating the series.

Software is not law or wealth planning, and success half the time is not enough to run a client's affairs. But **a capability that doubles every four months does not need to be finished to deserve a place in the plan.**

## It has started, and it is still clumsy

The first version of the staff is a chatbot and a client with a grievance.

The UK Financial Ombudsman Service reviewed a small sample of cases in early 2026 and estimated that AI contributed to up to a third (35 percent) of the responses to its initial assessments (FOS response to the FCA's Mills Review, February 2026). In the most extreme cases, it reports, representatives filed more than 200 pages in reply to a six page provisional decision. Chief ombudsman James Dipple-Johnstone told the Financial Times in October 2026 of one submission in which 37 of 39 references were incorrect.

In New Zealand, the Banking Ombudsman Scheme investigated 26 percent more disputes in 2025/26 than the year before (annual report media release, 2026). Ombudsman Nicola Sladden named financial pressure as the main cause, and added that customers increasingly use AI tools to prepare complaints: "these tools are giving users the confidence to pursue their complaints as far as possible."

For scale at home: the Swiss Banking Ombudsman closed 2'575 cases in 2025, of which 1'080 were written, 20 percent more than in 2024 (Annual Report 2025). Fraud was the most frequent cause. **The mediation body for the Swiss banks handles about 1'000 written cases a year.**

Today a bank can answer many AI written complaints by showing that they are wrong. That defence weakens with every model release. The errors will go. The excess length will go. **The persistence stays.**

## What the staff removes

Three quiet assumptions sit inside most client processes.

**The client gets tired.** The second reminder is never sent. The rejected claim is not appealed. The ombudsman is never contacted, because the form is long and the amount is small. A staff does not weigh effort against payoff, because its effort costs almost nothing. Every "we will come back to you" has a diary entry on the other side, and a reminder on the morning it expires.

**The client forgets.** The bank's memory of a ten year relationship is a CRM record written by three successive advisors. The client's assistant holds every statement, every email and its own note of every meeting. It can also ask for the bank's version. Under FinSA Art. 72 and 73, a client may at any time request a copy of the client file and of all other documents the bank created about them, in writing and free of charge, and the bank has 30 days to deliver. For investment advice, Art. 15 requires the bank to document the reasons for each recommendation that leads to a purchase or a sale. The request must come from the client or an authorised representative. The drafting, the diary and the reading of 400 pages can all be delegated. Few clients use this right today. A staff uses it as a matter of routine, with every provider.

**The bank is the expert in the room.** At the counter, on the phone and in the review meeting, the bank's employee usually knows more than the client. In this scenario the other side has read the general terms to the last clause, has [benchmarked every fee](/blog/when-every-client-negotiates-like-an-institution) and has [checked every transaction against the suitability profile](/blog/the-complaint-machine). The front line meets a better prepared counterparty at every contact.

Add [the volume](/blog/the-tsunami-is-not-coming-from-inside-the-bank) and the picture is complete: more requests, better founded, and no request ever abandoned.

## The same staff is your best client

A staff of 15 is demanding. It is also the most efficient counterparty a bank has ever had. It delivers complete files. It answers within the hour. It reads what you send. It knows what its principal wants and states it precisely.

A bank that is right the first time, fast and fully documented will find the staff easy to work with and cheap to serve. **The staff punishes one thing only: a process that works because the client does not look closely.**

## Run the experiment

Take one afternoon and one client journey. The exercise works for a bank, and just as well for an insurer, a pension fund, a telecom provider or a tax office.

Take the whole journey, and start it earlier than your process map does. A process map begins when the client contacts the bank. The journey begins weeks before, when the client puts a question to the staff: whether the house is affordable, who should manage an inheritance, whether the pension plan still fits. The staff researches broadly. It compares 30 providers where the client alone would have compared three (illustrative figures), reads their terms and fee schedules, and draws up a shortlist. **The bank is chosen or dropped before it knows the client exists.**

1. **Choose one journey, from the first need to the years after.** Buying a home, investing an inheritance, planning retirement, settling an estate. Start when the need appears and end well after the signature: the renewal, the fee change, the complaint, the exit.
2. **Walk the part you cannot see.** Put the client's opening question to an AI assistant today. Note whether your bank appears, what is said about it and which sources are used. Then list what an agent can learn about your offer without talking to a person. Whatever sits behind "contact us" is missing from the comparison, or filled in from someone else's data.
3. **Mark every step that depends on client fatigue.** Wherever the journey is cheaper for the bank because the client does not compare, does not ask, does not follow up or does not read, write it down.
4. **Multiply the inbound by ten and raise its quality.** Assume every letter is written at the level of your best external law firm. Note where the queue forms and who stands in it.
5. **Request your own file.** As a client of your own bank, ask for it: under FinSA Art. 72 for an advisory or investment relationship, under Art. 25 of the Data Protection Act (FADP) for a loan such as a mortgage, which FinSA does not cover. The deadline is 30 days in both cases, as a rule under the FADP. Count the days. Then read the file as an opposing expert would.
6. **Count the replies a person drafts from scratch.** Check whether two advisors would give the same answer to the same question.

The findings tend to point to the same redesign. Prices, terms and product facts published in a form an agent can read. Answers that are consistent and right the first time. Documentation produced as a side effect of the work and never reconstructed afterwards. Response times in hours. A structured channel through which a client's agent can reach the bank, and a reliable way to verify that it acts for the client. And the bank's own agents on the inside, with [escalation to people designed in](/blog/escalation-by-design), because **a staff of 15 per client cannot be met by an advisor with 200 clients.**

That is the case for an [agentic target operating model](/blog/agentic-tom-introduction), made from the client's side of the table.

The exercise, run on one journey from the first search to the succession: [The Staff of Fifteen Buys a House](/blog/the-staff-of-fifteen-buys-a-house).

## The cost of being wrong

If the scenario does not arrive, the experiment has cost an afternoon and left you with a client journey that is easier to find, faster, better documented and more consistent. If it arrives and you did not run the experiment, your complaint statistics will teach the same lessons at a higher price.

The staff of 15 may arrive later than this post suggests. **It will not arrive tired.**
