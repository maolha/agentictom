# The Tsunami Is Not Coming from Inside the Bank

Most of the conversation about agentic AI in banking focuses on what happens inside the institution. How agents will process loans, manage compliance, handle onboarding. That is the supply side. It matters. But it is not where the pressure arrives first.

The pressure arrives from outside. From customers — or more precisely, from the AI agents acting on their behalf.

## The mortgage example

Consider the Swiss mortgage market. Today, a customer looking for a mortgage contacts three to five banks. They compare offers manually. The process is slow, friction-heavy, and favours the bank with the existing relationship.

Now give that customer an AI agent.

The agent does not contact three banks. It contacts every bank simultaneously. One hundred or more requests, dispatched in seconds, each perfectly formatted, each carrying the same financial profile. The customer's effort is unchanged. The bank's inbound volume is not.

The numbers are not speculative. If the average customer moves from querying 3 banks to querying 100, every bank in the market sees its inbound application volume increase by roughly 30 times. The number of houses being bought has not changed. The number of applications has.

## The unit economics collapse

This is where the model breaks.

Today, a Swiss bank processes a mortgage application at a cost of roughly CHF 1,500. The win rate is approximately one in three. At CHF 20,000 revenue per mortgage, the expected value per application is around CHF 6,700. The unit economics are comfortable.

In an agentic world, the win rate drops to one in a hundred. The cost per application has not changed. The expected value per application falls to CHF 200. Every application you process but do not win costs you CHF 1,300.

To break even at the new volume and the new win rate, processing cost per application must fall by 97 percent. Not 10 percent. Not 50 percent. Ninety-seven.

That is not an efficiency programme. That is a different operating model.

## Speed becomes the product

There is a second dynamic. Research consistently shows that the first bank to respond captures a disproportionate share of conversions — some studies put it at 78 percent. Response time within five minutes yields conversion rates up to 100 times higher than responses after an hour.

When every customer has an AI agent that queries every bank at once, the bank that responds first wins. Not the bank with the best relationship. Not the bank with the lowest rate. The bank that responds first.

A human-driven process cannot respond in under five minutes to 30 times the current volume. Only an automated process can. Speed is no longer a service differentiator. It is a survival requirement.

## The inversion

Here is the part that should concern every head of mortgage lending in Switzerland: in this model, slow banks still process the applications. They spend the CHF 1,500. They run the credit checks. They prepare the offer. And then they lose. Consistently.

They become free price discovery tools for AI agents. They do the work. Someone else collects the revenue.

## What this means for the operating model

The mortgage example is illustrative, but the pattern generalises. Any product where customers can use AI agents to query multiple banks simultaneously will exhibit the same dynamics: volume explosion, win rate collapse, speed premium, and the inversion of who captures value.

This is why the agentic TOM is not a technology initiative. It is a structural response to a structural change in how demand reaches the bank. The customers are not changing what they want. They are changing how they ask for it. And that change alone is enough to break the model most Swiss banks currently operate.

The institutions that automate first will not just survive the volume. They will use it. Thirty times more inbound means thirty times more data, thirty times more pricing signal, thirty times more opportunity to learn. For the banks that can process at near-zero marginal cost and respond in seconds, the tsunami is not a threat. It is a moat.

For everyone else, it is a deadline.
