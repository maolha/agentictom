# The Staff of Fifteen Buys a House

On a bank's process map, a mortgage begins with an application and ends with a payout. For the couple in Winterthur it began a year earlier, with a search alert, and it ends when the house is sold or passed on, perhaps 30 years later. **The bank's map has four entries. The household's journey has twelve stages.**

[A Staff of Fifteen](/blog/a-staff-of-fifteen) closed with an exercise: take one client journey, start it before the first contact, and run it with a staff of specialists on the client's side. This post runs the exercise on the mortgage.

<!-- video:staff-of-fifteen-house -->

## The staff, assigned to one property

The earlier post names ten specialists: a lawyer, a tax adviser, an investment analyst, a wealth planner, a pension specialist, an insurance expert, a mortgage and property specialist, a negotiator, a strategist and a personal assistant. A property needs five more. A scout who watches every listing, all day. A valuer who checks the seller's price and the bank's. A building and energy expert. A marketer for the day the house is sold. A monitor who checks rates, the margin on the loan and the lender's conduct every day.

All 15 work for one household: the couple from [the Tsunami post](/blog/the-tsunami-is-not-coming-from-inside-the-bank), buying at CHF 1'200'000 with a mortgage of CHF 960'000 (illustrative figures).

## What the two maps show

**The loan tender is the loud part. The other stages are the larger change.** Today the bank hears from a mortgage client at purchase and at maturity. With a staff, contact arrives at every stage, for as long as the property is owned: a financing confirmation for each shortlisted property, a challenge to the margin every year, a tender at every maturity, an audit of the penalty at the sale. Closing is the one stage where the bank's workload falls.

**One in a hundred does not work at today's unit cost.** The Tsunami post modelled 30 lenders per buyer. A staff asks every lender it can reach, and MoneyPark already compares the offers of more than 100 banks, insurers and pension funds, so a hundred is a count of today's market. At about CHF 1'500 per application (the industry estimate used in the earlier post), a bank that wins one tender in a hundred spends CHF 150'000 on processing for each mortgage it books. The assumed lifetime value of that mortgage is CHF 20'000 (illustrative). The tender breaks even at CHF 200 per application.

**The margin leans on fatigue.** Comparis put the average published rate for a ten year fixed mortgage at 1.69 percent on 2 October 2025, and the best negotiated rate at 1.33 percent (Comparis, Zinsrabatte). On CHF 960'000 that gap is about CHF 3'460 a year. For scale, the average net interest margin of about 80 Swiss banks was 1.16 percent in 2025 (MoneyPark, Der Schweizer Hypothekarmarkt 2025). A staff never pays a published rate. It gives notice on the day after signing, obtains the formula for the early repayment penalty before it signs, and benchmarks the margin on a SARON loan every year.

**The advice meeting starts where it used to end.** The client arrives with a model: buying against renting over 30 years, the affordability test at retirement run ten years early, and the tax reform already priced in. From 1 January 2029 the imputed rental value of an owner occupied home is no longer taxed, and the deductions for upkeep and most debt interest go with it (Federal Council, 1 April 2026). The adviser adds something to that model, or is asked only for a price.

**The same staff is the cheapest client the bank has had**, on one condition: the bank can take a complete, structured file and answer it with a binding offer within hours.

## Four positions a lender can take

| Position | How it earns |
|---|---|
| The machine | Standard cases only. Binding offer within the hour, processing cost below CHF 200, sharp price. Earns on volume. |
| The specialist | Cases outside the standard: self employed income, an unusual property, a complex household. Judgement is the product, and few lenders answer at all. |
| The lifetime lender | Prices 30 years where others price the first ten. Wins on terms: a fair exit formula, free transfer to a new property or to heirs, a written position on retirement. |
| The middle | Days to answer, a standard product, a margin that relies on rollover. Its late offers serve the client as free price data, and its back book is tendered away one maturity at a time. |

Three of these are choices. The fourth is where a lender ends up without one.

## The file, for a mortgage

Step five of the exercise asks you to request your own file. For a mortgage the basis is the right of access in Art. 25 of the Data Protection Act (FADP): free of charge and as a rule within 30 days. FinSA Art. 72 covers financial services such as investment advice, so it applies where securities or pillar 3a investments are advised or pledged alongside the loan. Have Legal confirm the split for your bank.

Then read the file as the staff would. It looks for six things: the affordability calculation and the income it accepted, the valuation and its method, every exception to policy and who approved it, the discount and its reason, anything said about retirement, and every promise with a date.

## Five things to do first

1. **Find your break point.** Take your own cost per application and your win rate. Compute the number of lenders per tender at which your mortgage business stops earning.
2. **Pull last year's maturities.** Count how many renewed without a competing offer, and compare their price with new business on the same day.
3. **Request your own mortgage file.** Count the days, then read it against the six points.
4. **Send one case to five advisers.** Compare the rate, the terms and the answers on penalty, transfer and retirement.
5. **Describe the agent channel on one page.** What a structured request from a client's agent looks like, and how you verify that it acts for the client.
