# What Is an Agentic Target Operating Model?

Every bank has an AI strategy. Most of them answer the wrong question.

They ask: **where can we use AI?** The better question: what does the organisation look like when AI is not a tool but a participant?

That is what an Agentic Target Operating Model describes. Not a technology roadmap. **An organisational architecture** — who or what is accountable for which decisions, and how authority flows through a system that includes non-human actors.

## The distinction that changes everything

The word "agentic" is doing real work here. We are not talking about automation. Automation executes predefined rules on predefined inputs. It does what it is told.

An agent is different. It pursues goals. It operates in dynamic environments. **It makes decisions under uncertainty and learns from outcomes.**

That distinction changes how you govern it. A rule-based RPA bot can be governed like a piece of software. **An agent must be governed like a member of staff** — with onboarding, performance expectations, accountability structures, and an offboarding process.

If you are managing your agents like software, you are under-governing them. If you are trying to specify their behaviour in advance like automation, you are misunderstanding what they are.

## Three workforces, not one

The agentic TOM rests on a taxonomy. Not all non-human actors are the same.

**Bots** are the industrial backbone. Rule-based, auditable, deterministic. Reconciliations, extractions, routing. No judgment. By design.

**Agents** operate as junior-to-mid knowledge workers at scale. They pursue goals, use context, and make probabilistic decisions within a policy envelope defined by humans. When the stakes exceed their authorisation level, they [escalate by design](/blog/escalation-by-design).

**Humans** hold board-level decision proxies within operations. They own irreversible decisions, regulatory accountability, and relationship trust. They never touch routine.

The shift most organisations have not absorbed: **human roles concentrate at the edges** — exceptions, governance, relationships. Fewer humans, but each one more consequential.

## The end of the department

A conventional bank organises around functions: operations, compliance, risk, client service. Each function optimises its own process.

**Handoffs between functions are where most operational risk lives.** They are also where most of the cost is.

In an agentic TOM, the organising principle is not the function. **It is the outcome.** An agent system owns an end-to-end workflow — a loan decisioning process, a KYC remediation cycle, a client onboarding journey — and coordinates across what used to be functional boundaries.

The department does not disappear. But it stops being the unit of execution. It becomes the unit of governance.

## The first step is conceptual

The implications for leadership, governance, and technology are substantial. But the first step is not technical.

It is recognising that your bank already has a target operating model. It was designed for a workforce that was entirely human. **That assumption no longer holds.**

The question is not whether to redesign. It is whether you redesign deliberately — or discover, too late, that the model redesigned itself around you. For Swiss banks, the [structural advantages](/blog/swiss-banking-agentic-advantage) to move first already exist. And the [external pressure](/blog/the-tsunami-is-not-coming-from-inside-the-bank) is arriving faster than most assume.
