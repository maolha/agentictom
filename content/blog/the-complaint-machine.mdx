# The Complaint Machine

Here is something every head of compliance in Swiss banking knows but rarely says aloud: **the current volume of client complaints is not a measure of client satisfaction**. It is a measure of client effort.

Most clients do not complain. Not because they are satisfied, but because identifying what went wrong, gathering evidence, articulating the issue, and pursuing it through the bank's process costs more time and emotional energy than the likely outcome justifies. **The friction protects the bank.**

**AI agents remove the friction.**

## What an agent sees

Give a client's AI agent access to their portfolio history, transaction records, advisory documentation, and the risk disclosures they signed. Then give it a simple mandate: **identify anything that does not look right**.

The agent does not need to be adversarial. It needs to be thorough.

It compares portfolio performance against the stated benchmark and the agreed risk profile. It checks whether products sold match the suitability assessment on file. It identifies periods where the portfolio **deviated materially from the mandate without documented client consent**. It reviews fee changes and checks whether they were communicated in accordance with the contractual terms. It reads the risk disclosures and asks whether the risks that materialised were among those disclosed.

Each of these is a routine analytical task. Together, they constitute **a comprehensive review that most clients never perform** and most banks assume they never will.

## The volume problem

Today, a Swiss private bank might handle a few hundred formal complaints per year. The process is designed for that volume — manual review, relationship manager involvement, legal assessment, resolution.

Consider what happens when AI agents perform this analysis for a meaningful share of the client base. Not all clients will act on every finding. But the ones that do will arrive with **structured, evidenced complaints that are significantly harder to dismiss** than the vague dissatisfaction that characterises most complaints today.

The complaints will not be emotional. **They will be analytical.** "The portfolio underperformed the benchmark by 340 basis points over three years while operating outside the agreed risk parameters during months 14 through 19, without documented consent for the deviation." That is not a conversation the relationship manager is trained for.

## Suitability under a microscope

The area of greatest exposure is **suitability**. Swiss regulation requires that investment advice and discretionary management align with the client's risk profile, investment objectives, and financial situation. In practice, suitability assessments are performed at onboarding and reviewed periodically. Between reviews, products may be sold that stretch the definition of suitability without formally breaching it.

An AI agent does not stretch definitions. **It reads the suitability assessment and compares it against every transaction.** Literally every transaction. It flags the structured product that was sold to a conservative-profile client. It flags the concentration risk that built up gradually. It flags the FX exposure that was never part of the stated strategy.

Banks that have maintained rigorous suitability practices have nothing to fear. **Banks that have relied on the gap between what was documented and what the client would notice** have a problem that is about to surface.

## The rational response

The temptation will be to treat this as a legal risk to be managed — tighten complaint handling processes, prepare for higher volumes, engage more legal resource. **That is necessary but insufficient.**

The strategic response is to **get there first**. Audit your own book with the same rigour that an AI agent would apply. Identify suitability gaps before clients do. Proactively remediate where warranted. Contact clients with findings before their agents do.

**A bank that calls a client to say "we identified an issue in your portfolio and here is what we are doing about it" earns trust.** A bank that receives a structured complaint from an AI agent and responds defensively loses it.

## The broader implication

The complaint machine is a specific instance of a general pattern: **AI agents will enforce standards that the industry has historically relied on clients not to enforce**. Not because the standards did not exist, but because the cost of enforcement was borne by the client.

**When that cost falls to near zero, every gap between what was promised and what was delivered becomes visible.** The banks that have been disciplined will be validated. The banks that have relied on friction will be exposed.

This is not a future risk. The technology exists today. The question is when — not whether — clients begin deploying it. The banks that assume they have time are making the same bet they made about digital banking a decade ago.

**Preparation is cheaper than remediation. And significantly cheaper than litigation.**
